Accurate fault localization is critical for reducing outage duration and improving the reliability of model power distribution systems. Fault localization - identifying where a fault occurs, not merely detecting its presence is essential for rapid service restoration, yet remains difficult under partial observability, where only a limited number of micro-phasor measurement units ($\mu$PMUs) monitor networks containing hundreds of buses. Spatio-temporal graph neural networks(STGNNs), which is a combination of temporal sequence modeling with graph message passing over the topology, have shown strong localization accuracy in this setting. However their existing evaluations assume that every $\mu$PMUs is always available, as assumption as it fails in deployed systems where sensors are lost to communication faults, maintainance, or hardware failure. In this work, we reproduce the STGNN fault-localization benchmark of \cite{Karabulut2026} on the IEEE 123-bus-feeder. Using the same dataset, train-validation-test splits, and a class-imbalance aware training objective, we reproduce the performance of GRU, RGCN, RGSAGE-Mean, RGSAGE-Max, and RSATv2 models, achieving a soft-voting macro-F1 score of [X], consistent with the reported range. Building on this reproduced benchmark, we perform the first systematic evaluation of STGNN robustness to $\mu$PMU loss by removing $k \in \{0,1,2,5,10\}$ of the 25 available $\mu$PMUs  during inference.Our experiments show that localization performance [degrades sharply / degrades gradually] as sensor availability decreases, with high-impedance faults located far from instrumented buses exhibiting the largest performance degradation. To mitigate this, we introduce a simple, architecture-independent dropout-aware training, a simple, architecture-agnostic scheme that randomly masks measurement nodes while training which inturn encourages the model to learn representations that are less dependent on specific sensors. Without introducing any additional inference-time computation, the proposed apporach recovers [X] of the localization accuracy lost under sensor dropout relative to standard training. All comparisons use identical data and evaluation protocols, so reported effects are internally consistent. Our results indicate that incorporating sensor-dropout awareness during training substantially improves the resilience of STGNN-based fault localization, bringing such models closer to reliable deployment in sparsely instrumented grids.
